# Dialogue, Off-Screen Voices and Camera Patterns

## Fields for multi-person dialogue

For every dialogue beat, record:

- `speaker`: the person actually speaking.
- `camera_subject`: the main subject on screen, which can be the listener.
- `trigger_event`: the word, sound, action or look that triggers the change in performance.
- `evidence_source`: where the audience sees or hears the change: face, body, voice, prop or spatial relationship.
- `information_priority`: what the audience must understand first at this moment.

## Common structures

### The listener shot

The speaker keeps talking off screen or at the edge of the frame while the camera stays on the listener. The listener keeps doing their task, and only pauses or changes breathing after the trigger word arrives; avoid turning the head early.

### Being interrupted

Write the interrupted person's unfinished word or unfinished action, the exact point where the interrupter comes in, and whether the interrupted person swallows the rest of the sentence, shifts their gaze or carries on with the task in their hands.

### No dialogue

Trigger with a sound or an object change that can be located: a lock clicking softly, a short phone vibration, distant footsteps stopping, water trembling in a cup. Keep the camera on the person reacting; no explanatory line is needed.

## Camera intent template

```text
Camera intent: at this moment the audience needs to feel ____, so the camera sits ____; it holds still until ____, then moves along ____ at ____ speed for ____ seconds, and stops at ____, so that ____'s reaction can play out in full.
```

If a move has no clear trigger and no reason to stop, delete the move.

## What camera position means emotionally

- Eye-level medium close-up: does not judge for the audience; good for reading restrained reactions.
- Slightly low angle: can create control or pressure, but must fit the relationship; it does not mechanically mean "powerful".
- Slightly high angle: can show exposure or isolation, or may simply be spatial observation; write the situational reason.
- Behind and to the side, or partly blocked: creates distance, an eavesdropping feeling, or a relationship the character cannot truly close.
- Two-shot: stresses a change in the relationship, who moves closer or who withdraws; watch the axis and eye lines.
- Reaction close-up: only after the trigger; going in early gives the emotion away too soon.

## Minimum parameters for a move

Keep only the parameters that affect how the performance reads:

1. The trigger.
2. The path or direction.
3. Speed, and whether it is constant.
4. Duration.
5. Where it stops.

Focal length, aperture, shutter and equipment models are usually left out, unless the user asks for them or they change how readable the performance is.
